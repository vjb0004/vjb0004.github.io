Advising as Pedagogy | ENGL 7870

This project argues that academic advising functions as a pedagogical space where students develop academic literacies, practice rhetorical decision-making, and learn to transfer knowledge across contexts. Drawing on theories of learning transfer and multiliteracies, I reposition one-on-one advising appointments as instructional encounters rather than merely administrative exchanges. By analyzing my own advising practice through the lens of technical and professional communication, I make visible the often-overlooked instructional labor advisors perform in fostering student autonomy and applied learning.

Multilingual Survey Client Design Project | ENGL 7080

This project involved co-designing a multilingual, user-centered digital survey for an adult ESOL program, with the goal of reducing linguistic, cultural, and emotional barriers to participation. Grounded in participatory design and social justice frameworks, the survey positioned multilingualism as an asset rather than a deficit and prioritized clarity, trust-building, and accessibility in both translation and interface design. Through iterative usability testing, client negotiation, and application of the Planned Rapid Document Development model, this project demonstrates how ethical document design can support linguistic equity and meaningful data collection.

Making Sense of SAP | ENGL 7010

My SAP study on federal financial aid communication analyzes how institutions present Satisfactory Academic Progress policies to students, examining clarity, tone, accessibility, and rhetorical design in order to evaluate how effectively these high-stakes policies are communicated and how they shape students’ understanding of risk, responsibility, and institutional support.

A Multi-Vocal Exploration of Advising Pedagogy | ENGL 7040

This project presents a fictionalized, annotated advising scenario designed to demonstrate how pedagogical theory can be intentionally applied within one-on-one advising interactions. Drawing on multiliteracies pedagogy, transfer of learning, multimodality, and Universal Design for Learning (UDL), I illustrate how advisors can use repetition, reinforcement, and multimodal supports to facilitate student autonomy and deeper learning.

Coordinated Minor: Language, Literacy, & Accessibility Studies

How my research integrates language, learning, and inclusive communication design

My coordinated minor in Language, Literacy, and Accessibility Studies brings together my interests in linguistics, rhetoric, technical communication, and education. Across my coursework and projects, I examine how language functions as both a cognitive and social tool—and how communication practices can either support or hinder access, understanding, and participation.

This minor supports my broader goal of designing and analyzing communication systems that are equitable, usable, and responsive to diverse audiences, particularly in educational, rural, and agricultural contexts. My work is grounded in the belief that accessibility is not an add-on, but a core component of effective communication.

Primary Areas of Focus

  • Language acquisition and literacy development in children and adult learners
  • Language ideologies and power dynamics in educational and institutional settings
  • Accessibility, plain language, and Universal Design for Learning (UDL)
  • Multimodal and digital communication for instructional and public-facing contexts
  • Technical communication for rural, agricultural, and first-generation communities

Through this coordinated minor, I have explored topics such as curriculum and instructional design, accessible document creation, media and risk communication, and the role of language in shaping identity and belonging. These interests also inform my teaching and advising work, where I prioritize clarity, inclusivity, and audience-centered design.

Collectively, my research reflects an interdisciplinary approach that bridges technical communication, linguistics, literacy studies, and accessibility research—preparing me for future doctoral study and work at the intersection of language, learning, and social justice.
